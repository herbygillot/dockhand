# The whole-tree survey, and the stubs it miscounted

A whole-tree `assess --all` over 41,730 ports, 4 hours 35 minutes at 711% CPU, reported 81.1% input-found, 16.9% unsupported, and 2.1% unknown, against 39.3% input-found on 2026-09-17. The headline was low, and for a reason that was a bug in the survey rather than a limit of the editor.

One finding accounted for 4,675 of the 7,896 ports not covered, 59% of the remainder, and every instance said the same thing: the indexed subport was not the port evaluation selected. Assessed on its own, each such port was ready. `MoltenVK` reported an input at line 10; six drawn at random from the bucket, all `py-` and `p5-` names, reported theirs. Across the whole bucket the evaluated port was a versioned sibling of the indexed one, `p5-acme-lolcat` to `p5.34-acme-lolcat`, in 96.6% of cases and a suffixed subport, `MoltenVK` to `MoltenVK-latest`, in another 0.7%. That is the shape stub redirection produces, and it is the redirection working.

The survey selects every port by its indexed name, and a stub's indexed name is the stub. The probe redirects a stub to the subport carrying its release and records the stub's name on its request, but did not say so, and the survey's consistency check compared the indexed name against the evaluated one and called the difference unsupported. Explicit selection never hit it because an explicit name is not carried as an indexed one. The probe now exposes the stub it resolved, and the check accepts a disagreement exactly when the indexed name is that stub; a disagreement with no stub behind it, `SuiteSparse` evaluating as `SuiteSparse_config`, is still refused, since that is a port the index did not name.

The rule is a pure function with a test that states both halves. The bulk path was reproduced on the ruby category, 1,399 ports through the same index-driven selection in 73 seconds: the 244 of them that had been in the mismatch bucket are all input-found, and none report the mismatch.

Setting the bucket aside, the tree stands at roughly 92% input-found, and what remains is about 3,200 ports: 1,154 with no editable version input established, 955 on unsupported fetch conventions split between metaports with no archives and pre-fetch hooks with unrecognized behavior, 646 whose probe depends on host filesystem state, 130 genuine index disagreements, and tails of calculated checksum algorithms, dependency manifests, and unknown tag conventions. The survey's results stay out of the checkout.
