# 2026-10-01: the waits with no bound (batch 26, second part)

Batch 26 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md), its second part: the waits the limits sweep found with no bound, and a body that stalls after its headers ([limits](../limits.md)). How long a build may run was the person's to say, and they chose 6 hours, configurable, GitHub's own cap on a job (D16).

## What changed

- **A body that stalls is given up.** `fetch.Transport` bounded a response's headers at a minute, and nothing after: GitHub's API, a job's log, PyPI, and the mirror's index could each hold their caller on a host that sent its headers and stopped. Each body now goes 3 minutes without a byte at most (`fetch.BodyStall`), as a download does, and goes on at any pace short of that.
- **A call into the Portfile evaluator is answered within 5 minutes** (`eval.CallBound`), or its interpreter is ended and the session broken (`rpc.WithCallBound`, `rpc.ErrNoAnswer`). One takes well under a second; a Portfile running a command that never returns held its caller as long as it ran.
- **A portindex run ends at an hour** (`portindex.RunBound`). A full pass takes 3 to 4 minutes here, across 22 recorded, and an incremental one 17 seconds at most. Another process waiting on the index's lock waits as long as that run, since a process that ends lets go of its lock: the waits on file locks are bounded by their holders' work, now bounded itself.
- **A Tart build ends at 6 hours, and lint at 10 minutes** (D16). A target's dependencies' installs, fetch, checksum, and install share the bound (`providers.tart.build_timeout`), which the guest program counts down; one past it is ended and fails at the phase it was in, "the build ran past its 6h bound (providers.tart.build_timeout), so it was ended". The guest's `timed`, which ended only `port`, now signals what `port` started first, the compilers a build runs, which outlived it, and waits up to 10 seconds for them rather than always 10. A fetch's transfers are MacPorts' own to bound, which drops one below its speed for a minute. The guest program's pin moves; its protocol stays 2, since a build that passed before passed within the bound.
- **A GitHub Actions run is cancelled past its bound** (`providers.github.build_timeout`, 6 hours), and GitHub's cancelling waited 10 minutes. MacPorts' workflow sets `timeout-minutes: 0`, so GitHub's own 6-hour cap on a hosted job is the one that applies; a job that ran to it, or that dockhand cancelled for its bound, is read as ended for its time, and the port it was installing fails, "on build (macos-15): GitHub ended the job at its 6h0m0s cap on a job". Where it was read before, as a run with no port built, it was an infrastructure failure, tried three times, 18 hours.
- **A port whose install never ended isn't read as passed.** The workflow marks only a failure, so a port read as passed once its install began: a job ended mid-install, at GitHub's cap or cancelled, passed the port it was building. Its install has ended once its group closes (`Built.Installed`), and one that began and never did is cut short (`Built.CutShort`): no verdict, or failed where the job was ended for its time.
- **Setup waits 30 minutes in all on an ASIF VM blocking Tart's listing,** then says to stop it and run setup again, where it waited as long as the VM ran.

## Decided along the way

- **The command provider's script keeps no bound.** It's the person's own, which D16 doesn't name; a cancel still stops it.
- **The wait for a free Tart VM keeps no bound.** dockhand's own VMs end with their builds now; the person's own are theirs, and the wait is said.
- **A guest's file, and git's blobs, still read whole,** as the first part decided, and a kept archive is still signed in memory, the person's to choose.
