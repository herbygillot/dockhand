# 2026-10-01: build history kept by weight (batch 29)

Batch 29 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md), D6 as the person decided it on 2026-10-01: one `cleanup.after`, of 15 days, for everything cleanup removes; logs compressed with gzip; open branches keeping everything their evidence reads; and reuse keeping one archive per port, environment, and variant set. Measured that day: logs 802 MB across 62 checks, kept archives 2.4 GB in 140, the largest 214 to 287 MB, the database 3 MB.

## What changed

- **`cleanup.after` defaults to 15 days,** where it was 7, for caches, events, logs, and what ended branches' checks recorded alike (`config.DefaultCleanupAfter`).
- **A check's logs are kept compressed** once it ends (`buildlog.Compress`), and cleanup compresses what it finds still written, a check that ended before this build's among them. `logs`, the failure summary's cause finder, and every path dockhand shows or gives in JSON read a log by the path it was written at, compressed or not (`buildlog.Open`, `buildlog.Where`). One log of 13.4 MB was 0.83 MB under gzip -9; Go writes gzip, where it only reads bzip2.
- **Logs are removed by what they stand for** (`engine.cleanLogs`): an ended branch's once it has been merged, closed, or archived for `cleanup.after`; an open branch's where they count for nothing, superseded for `cleanup.after`. An open branch keeps the logs of its newest check in each environment it has built in, and of its three newest. A check still going is left as it is.
- **A branch records when it ended** (schema 28, `branches.ended_at`, `model.Branch.EndedAt`): the moment it leaves open, and none once it opens again. One that ended before the schema is taken to end at the migration, so nothing it recorded goes at once.
- **What an ended branch recorded of its checks goes** once it has been ended for `cleanup.after` (`store.Tx.PruneHistory`): its checks' results, provider runs, plans, and revisions, and its assessments, in the order foreign keys allow, then the recorded inputs no result names. Kept: its newest check, which status shows as it ended, without what it built (`BranchStatus.Pruned`); every result reuse may still choose, the newest passed build of its target in its environment that recorded what it read, from the build rather than a reuse, with its provider run and check; and a provider run a remaining result reused, which evidence names. `logs check-12` for one that's gone says it's no longer recorded, rather than that there was no such check, and `logs` of a kept check with nothing left says why.
- **Kept archives are bounded by count** (`PruneArchives`): an archive stays while it's the archive of the newest passed build of a port, on an environment, with its variants, that reuse may choose, or of an open branch's newest passed result of a port on an environment; an older build's goes, however recent. A one-hour grace keeps one a build has just kept. Reuse already passes over a build whose archive isn't kept, so nothing it reads breaks. A port the size of rust, checked on six releases, now keeps about 1.7 GB, where five newest results each would have kept about 8.6 GB.
- **An open branch's assessments of a tree it moved past** go 7 days after it did (`PruneAssessments`); a branch restored to that tree is assessed again.
- **Cleanup says what it did,** by kind and size, in the journal, in `cleanup.log`, and from serve: "compressed 242 logs, 802 MB to 50 MB; removed 12 checks' logs, 140 MB, kept for nothing past 15 days".

## Decided along the way

- **An open branch's evidence is approximated, for its logs,** by its newest check in each environment it has built in, whatever tree: computing each open branch's evidence would read its worktree. A branch restored to an older tree may then find an older check's logs gone, superseded, while its results stand.
- **Log directories with no check named for them are left.** One database serves several checkouts, whose checks' logs share `~/.dockhand/logs` by name.
- **Recorded inputs need no bound of their own,** about 6 KB a build; they go with the last result naming them.
