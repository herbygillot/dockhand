# One place that knows the shape of an if

A survey for Tcl syntax logic outside `internal/tcl/syntax` found the parser genuinely central, used by eight packages and reimplemented by none, and three grades of scatter around it. This change takes the first two.

The first was three copies of how an `if` lays out its words. `portedit/unmodeled.go` had `controlParts`, which also knew `foreach`, `while`, `catch`, and `for`; the candidate scanner in `portfile/version.go` had its own `then`/`elseif`/`else` state machine and, since this morning, its own `switch` walker, written by hand and wrong on the first try; and `eval/fetch.go`'s `conditionalRejection` had a third. They are one method now, `Command.Control`, in the parser's package, and it knows `switch` in both spellings, the braced list and the inline pairs, with patterns as selectors and a `-` body passed over. An `if` still waiting for a condition is not a control at all, which is stricter than `controlParts` was and what the rejection grammar needed anyway.

Two callers widened slightly by taking the shared shape, and both widenings are the consistent rule rather than accidents. The candidate scanner now descends `while`, `catch`, and `for` bodies as it always did `foreach` and `if`; every control's bodies are scripts. And the host-state analysis now treats a `switch` on a dimension as it treats an `if` on one, judging its arms as guarded branches, where before a `switch` reading an unmodeled dimension was refused outright as an ordinary command reading it. Nothing in the suite depended on either difference.

The second grade was six small helpers living where they were first needed. `scriptCommands`, `commandWords`, and `messageSegments` from `eval` are `Script.Direct`, `Command.Is`, and `Word.Plain`; `dependency`'s `literalWords` keeps its token policy and takes the literal-argument part from `Command.LiteralArgs`; the manual brace and quote stripping in the candidate scanner is `Word.Inner`, which reads the segment rather than the first and last byte; and `rewriteLiteralDeclaration` moved from `portedit`'s git source file to `portfile` beside the other declaration edits, under the same `ErrUnsupported`, which `portedit` already aliased. The MacPorts Base hook wrapper prefix that `fetch.go` cut three times is one `hookBody`. A `foreach` variable list is split with `ListValues` instead of `strings.Fields`.

Left for a separate change, and named in the survey: `UniqueLiteral`'s byte-level `boundary` and `inComment`, which search raw text for a token where the scanner would not descend, and the two sites that look inside `expr` conditions by hand, `pureCondition` and the Darwin profile reader, which exist because the parser reads scripts and lists but not expressions.
