# 2026-09-22: when a line happened, and how long a phase took

Two additions after the day's two exercise bumps, git-devel on Tart and
trufflehog on GitHub, each of which was watched through a terminal that
could say neither when its lines were printed nor how long the phases
had taken; the git-devel run's interesting number, thirty minutes of
tests inside a fifty-minute verification, came from reading the guest's
log by hand.

**`--timestamps`.** A global output flag. In text mode it wraps the
command's stderr in a writer that stamps the start of every line with
the wall clock to the second, so progress reports and the command's own
lines, the preview's diff header, a warning, a phase transition, all
carry the same clock; a line written in pieces is stamped once. In JSON
mode the reports carry a `time` field, RFC 3339, so their lines stay
parseable. It is opt-in, and nothing else about the lines changes.

**Durations from the record.** The record already held most of what a
duration needs: a job's acceptance and finish, an attempt's creation and
its verdict's observation, a publication's confirmation. It did not hold
when the branch was integrated, the end of the preparation phase;
`PreparedChange.IntegratedAt` holds it now, set in the integration
transaction, a field on a JSON-stored struct and no migration. On that,
`workflow/view` gains `Took`, a duration in a person's words, "8s",
"2m14s", "1h02m", "under 1s", and `Phases`, a job's finished phases in
order with the whole job last, and `PhaseWords`, the one line a finished
job's narration ends with: "took 34m12s: preparation 2m14s, verification
31m40s, publication 12s". The branch and verdict milestones carry their
own "in 2m14s", the `-v` stream's terminal line carries the same words,
and `status -v` lists the phases under `took:`. A running job lists what
has finished, and a reattached command says the same numbers as the one
that watched, since none of them come from the terminal's clock.

Tests: the projection's words at fixed times, including a running job
and an unknown verdict; the CLI's stamped lines in text and the `time`
field in JSON; the integration time on the record after a durable
preparation; and the `took` lines in a finished job's narration and in
`status -v`.
