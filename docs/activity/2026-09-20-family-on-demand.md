# The family, only when something needs it

Assessing `php84` took 42 seconds, and the interpreter was not why: dockhand's evaluation of the port costs what MacPorts' own does, 20 to 30 milliseconds untraced and under a quarter of a second in a fresh traced process on a modeled platform. The count was why. A subport selection was re-resolved to its owning Portfile's main port and the baseline evaluated every subport of it, 605 for php, 14 seconds; then each of the 7 modeled contexts evaluated all 605 again in its own interpreter, 27 seconds. The 27 candidate probes, which already evaluated the selected port alone, cost 0.7 seconds between them.

Two things consumed those evaluations. `assessArchives` reads one entry of each observation, the selected port's, so its contexts now evaluate that port alone; one boolean, since `context` already shaped a target from it for the probes. The baseline is now the selected port's own evaluation, plus the recorded stub's entry when there is one, since the stub check and the borrowed livecheck read it. A main-port selection still evaluates its Portfile whole at load, because stub detection reads the siblings and an edit there is what they share. A subport's siblings are evaluated on first demand by `familySnapshot`, through the same shared session, and kept.

Who demands them is the fidelity checks, and they must: `ReleaseScope`, `ScopedVersion`, `Revision`, `GitVersion`, and the dependency regeneration's port-set comparison exist to show that an edit changed nothing but what it meant to, and a sibling that moved is exactly what they look for. All seven sites read the family now; a probe that edits nothing compares the selected port with itself. One flow rebases the baseline: dependency regeneration copies the input and sets `before` to the family evaluated with the dependency block stripped, so the regenerated declarations are compared against that; the family pointer follows the rebase, which the preparation tests caught when it did not. A flag saying "assessment only" would have been the wrong shape, since a probe built for assessment can be handed on to an edit; the lazy family has no mode to get wrong, and a site missed would fail the port-set length check loudly rather than pass quietly.

Measured with the old and new binaries interleaved on the same loaded machine: php84 43.0 and 43.2 seconds before, 7.8 and 8.0 after; jq 7.7 and 7.1 before, 7.4 and 6.9 after, unchanged within noise, as a port with no siblings should be. Three tests count evaluations through the shared session: a subport baseline is one selected evaluation and no family until asked, then one family kept; a main port's baseline is its family; a recorded stub joins a subport baseline as a second selected evaluation. From the PortIndex, 64% of the tree's ports live in a Portfile with siblings, so what php showed at the extreme every family pays in proportion; the whole-tree survey's evaluation count falls about fifteenfold. A bump still evaluates the family once for fidelity, and its plan observations still cover the family, since those are the checks that make the edit trustworthy.

Not done: reusing one interpreter across modeled observations. It would save about half a second on every port, siblings or not, and observation setup mutates the interpreter it runs in, so it needs a reset protocol rather than a cache. Not worth its disruption for that gain.
