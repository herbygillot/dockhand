# Three readers on the expression parser

The three readers that looked inside conditions by hand now read the tree `ParseExpr` gives them, and the parser package gained the one fact they all needed: which of a command's words are expressions. `Command.Expressions` names the windows, the body of an `if`, `while`, or `for` condition, and `expr`'s argument, which is its one braced word's body or the span across its bare words since Tcl joins those before evaluating; `Command.Reads` lists what a command reads, the variables of its expressions and the substitutions of every other word, with bodies left to be walked as the commands they hold. An expression that does not parse contributes the substitutions of its text instead, so a read is never missed for want of a grammar.

The host-state analysis in `unmodeled.go` matched `${os.version}` and its kin with a regex over raw text, and compiled a regex per tainted variable on every check. Its dimension is a predicate on a variable's name now, applied to what `Reads` returns, so `$os.version` is what Tcl says it is, the variable `os` followed by text, and a read inside a command substitution is found by structure rather than by pattern.

The Darwin profile scanner in `profiles.go` matched comparisons with five regexes and `comparisonEnd`, a heuristic about what may follow an operand. It walks comparison nodes now: one side the `os.major` read, the other a number, a variable, or `[option name]`, or else refused. Parentheses and `&&` chains cost nothing, `[vercmp ${os.major} >= 18]` is an argument list, and `expr` with bare arguments is parsed as the one expression it is. Its five tests kept their contract and gained six cases the regexes could not state. One guard from the old code was nearly lost and is kept on purpose: a form the parser does not model that reads the Darwin major, `cgit`'s ternary over `${os.major} <= 8`, is refused rather than counted as a harmless read, because a comparison selects between contexts and observing only the native one could miss the other side. `cgit` stays unknown, with a message that now says why.

`pureCondition` in `fetch.go` scanned brackets byte by byte and refused nesting outright. It checks each `Call` node as a parsed command: one command, a pure variant query by name, bare literal arguments. Grouping and braced text are simply grammar now, and a form the parser does not model is refused.

Two honest limits stayed where they were. A braced word of an ordinary command is still walked as a possible script, since a `subport`, a `variant`, and a hook body are exactly that and nothing structural tells them from `notes` text; the scanner is conservative there as before. And the reads test's claim about braced text was narrowed to what the code guarantees. The suite is green; `jq`, `p5.34-text-soundex` through the rejection grammar, and `php84` through the host-state analysis assess exactly as before.
