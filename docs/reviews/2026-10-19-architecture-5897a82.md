# Architecture review: main from 959026d to 5897a82

herbygillot/dockhand, `main @ 5897a82`, read 2026-10-19. 146 commits since the 2026-10-15 second scan (959026d): 452 files, +18,578 −7,484. The tree builds and vets. Every file:line below was read at 5897a82.

## The short version

The question was whether the fixes since the last review were made without high-level consideration of the architecture. The answer is: the layering was considered, in every batch; the ownership was not, in about a dozen of them.

**Layering held.** No package below the engine gained an import of engine, command or store. The rule in internal/command/doc.go (eligibility, guardrails, multi-op failure rules and state belong in the engine) was followed by each fix: the rule went to the engine, with a typed value and in most cases a test. The concepts the 2026-10-15 review drew landed as drawn: `engine.Select`, `CheckStanding`, `Where` and `Profile`, `Covered` and `PlanCovers`, `ProposeCheck`, typed concerns, the readings on `model.Plan`, `Session`, `CleanupIfDue`, `BaselineComparison`, `RefreshResult`. Thirteen of the second scan's sixteen relocations landed, five of its ten engine-owned rules, all of its folds, and the deleted surface (update's composite flags, submit's, queue, watch, auth, init, providers, restore, clean --archived) is gone cleanly, with two drifted strings left behind.

**What slipped is one pattern, repeated.** A rule was moved to the engine and its old copy at the call site stayed, or a new copy was written beside it a day later. Four decisions now have two owners: the branch a port's edit goes in (`engine.Select` beside command/ship.go:147-175 and author.go:821-842), whether a check stands for this ask (`CheckNeeded` and `Covered` glued at command/submit.go:254), whether this may go on unattended (`HeldForLook`, `AppliesUnasked`, `!Unambiguous()` and a round trip through the terminal), and which run is the branch's active check (`ActiveCheck`'s filter against `BranchStatus.Active` with none). ship.go, the largest new file, has its own 0/1/many branch rule written the day after `Select` took it, a third copy of the check pipeline, and a batch failure rule; and serve's path holds a major version twice. None of these is a layering error. They are second owners, and second owners drift: the rc4 fix for `ship --mine` put the no-empty-branch rule at the command's call site (`unattended || batch`, command/ship.go:335) while the engine's `Ship` hard-codes it (engine/ship.go:94).

**Below the engine** the fixes added five concept-level duplicates: "a failure another try may not meet" now has five shapes and two classifiers; the "older than this Mac's MacPorts" sentence is composed three times; the build-profile vocabulary lives in config and in engine; the Tart 2.39.0 minimum is refused twice; the two-minute liveness window is defined in store and in coord. One layering edge is new: `planning`, which docs/architecture.md says reads nothing, imports `macos` to compose a sentence that it later parses back into a bool.

**One finding changes behaviour.** `BranchStatus.Active` counts baseline runs and runs asked to stop, so `CheckStanding()` says Checking, and serve's `PassingBranches` waits, while a baseline of a passed check runs (engine/status.go:315 against runner.go:51 and :105). It needs a test and one predicate.

**The numbers.** The engine grew 2,350 lines (+14%), about 1,000 of them moves and 1,350 new rules; its exported func and type lines went 311 to 387. Command is 11,090 lines, no smaller than before the deletions: the thousand lines they freed went to ship.go (476), the selector rollout and status's fold of queue and watch. Command's engine surface went 532 to 602 names. Two-way file pairs went 35 to 33, with ship↔submit new. `t.Parallel` is still 2. docs/architecture.md was not updated for any of it.

**What to do.** One "second owner" batch now, before X1: about sixteen deletions of a few lines each, with a test apiece, listed in section 5. The structural ones (one gate for ship and serve, one consent value, the check pipeline once, attention on the standing) stay in v0.5.0 part 4.1 where the roadmap places them, with two additions to its text. And a counter-proposal on ship: the roadmap keeps two drivers because serve needs none of the command's resume, follow or commit binding. The reverse direction has no such cost: the command's ship calls `Engine.Ship` for edit, tidy and check, and keeps resume, follow and binding around it. The 95 lines of judgments in ship.go are what two drivers cost today, and they will grow with each fix. Process-wise, a relocation batch should end with a grep for the old rule's shape, and its note should list the call sites that now call the engine's rule; the batch notes name the rule they moved but not the copies they left.

Every batch since 959026d has an activity note tying it to a review row, and every one puts the rule it names in the engine. What the notes don't say is which callers now call it, and that is where the copies survive. The four two-owner decisions in the short version share one history: the engine function was added in one batch (7decf60, 07ce5b1, ef7b92a, 1306598), the callers the batch touched were switched, and a caller it didn't touch, or a new caller written the next day, kept judging for itself. ship.go is the clearest case: written in the same week as `Select`, it re-derives the branch (R3-3), the consent (R3-9), the check pipeline (R3-4) and the publication gate (R3-10) rather than calling the four engine rules that had just been built for it.

The roadmap's answer is part 4.1, three parts and about fourteen batches away: a `branchstate` verdict replaces the six separate judgments and "one gate for ship and serve" makes the command's ship call `goOn`'s replacement. That is the right end state for attention, the standing, the consent value and the gate. It is the wrong wait for the copies that are a few lines each: they will be read and patched by every fix between now and then, and the rc4 fix already showed the drift (R3-11).

On ship itself, the roadmap keeps two drivers: "serve needs none of the command's resume, follow, or commit binding." That is true, and it argues against serve calling the command's driver, which nobody proposed. The other direction costs nothing: `Engine.Ship` already runs edit → tidy → check with `goOn`'s holds; the command's `shipOne` can call it for those stages and keep its resume (`runQueued`), follow (`PlanCovers`) and commit binding (`submitPassed`) around it. What the command's ship would lose is its own copies of the branch rule, the consent switch, the go-on rules and the check pipeline, which is what section 2 lists. The done criterion is measurable: zero judgment lines in command/ship.go. I'd put this in part 4.1's "one gate" bullet as its shape, and pull the branch rule and the check pipeline forward into the batch below, since they don't depend on the verdict.
